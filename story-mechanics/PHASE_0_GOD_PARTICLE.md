# Phase 0 — God Particle

## State of Being

You are not a god yet. You are a *remnant* — a fragment of divine will too small to be perceived, too stubborn to fully dissolve. You have no name you can remember. You have no form. You cannot interact with living things. Animals do not feel you. People walk through the area where you drift and feel nothing beyond a vague unease, if that.

You are beneath the threshold of life, closer in character to geology than to theology.

But the ancient world remembers you in its bones.

Trees that have stood for centuries carry traces of old devotion in their rings — not worship, exactly, more like the residue of attention directed at something for a long time. Standing stones were placed with intention, and that intention was directed at *you*. Sacred springs were sacred because you were once reflected in them clearly enough that people noticed. The oldest rocks still hold the impression of prayers pressed into them over millennia, the way a pillow holds the shape of a head long after the sleeper has gone.

You feed on these traces. You are, in the most literal sense, *remembering yourself*.

---

## What You Can and Cannot Do

**You cannot:**
- Interact with any living creature — people, animals, birds
- Be perceived by any living thing (they feel nothing)
- Use miracles or abilities
- Build structures
- Access the ascension tree (it doesn't exist yet)

**You can:**
- Move freely through the landscape
- Absorb scattered divine energy (glowing residue orbs)
- Dwell near ancient landmarks to draw energy and memory from them
- Explore — permanently peeling back the fog of war
- Feel the shape of the world: terrain slows you in forests, you float slightly above mountains

The constraint is not a limitation to be frustrated by. It is the *point*. You experience the world the way it was before you had followers — as pure sensation, pure geography. The fog of war makes the map feel genuinely unknown. The inability to rush toward people means you have to move through the world rather than across it.

---

## Energy Sources

Energy comes from two sources: residue orbs scattered across the landscape, and the ancient landmarks hidden in the fog.

### Residue Orbs
Glowing purple fragments of dissolved divinity, visible once the surrounding fog is cleared. Plentiful but individually modest.

- Each gives 3–7 energy
- Absorbed automatically on close approach
- Density is higher near landmarks (unconsciously breadcrumbing the player)
- Approximately 26 orbs scattered across the map

### Ancient Landmarks
Rare, significant, and each tied to a memory. These require real exploration to find.

| Landmark | Energy | Requirement |
|----------|--------|-------------|
| Each Standing Stone (×5) | ~12 | Approach and dwell briefly |
| The Ancient Oak | ~8 | Approach — it recognises you |
| The Sacred Spring | ~15 | Find it (hidden in a valley fold) |
| The Drowned Temple | ~20 | Find it (partially submerged, deepest fog) |
| The High Cairn | ~10 | Reach it (mountain approach terrain) |

**Total available energy:**
- Orbs alone: ~130 (more than enough)
- Landmarks alone: ~105 (just barely enough with all found)
- Combined: well over 200, allowing for exploration at different paces

The energy cap is 100. Surge (ascension node, Phase 1) adds 60 more — but that comes later.

## The Five Standing Stones — *The Shattered Name*

Your name was broken when you dissolved. It shattered into five syllables, each crystallising inside one of the ancient standing stones that your earliest worshippers placed across the land. The stones are older than the village. Older than farming. They were placed by people who communed with you before they had language sophisticated enough to name what they were communing with.

Each stone is unique — different height, different weathering, different placement in the landscape. What unites them is the quality of their silence when you approach: a stillness that feels withheld rather than empty.

When you dwell near a standing stone, it releases its stored memory. The stone goes dark — energy spent, syllable returned. You hear (feel, experience — it isn't really hearing) a fragment of your name and receive a memory of the world as you once knew it.

**Finding at least 3 of 5 stones is required to become a Whisper.** You need enough of yourself to coalesce. A name with two syllables is not enough. Three is a threshold. Four is coherent. Five is complete.

### Stone Locations (seeded to terrain)
All positions are fixed at world generation — the same every playthrough.

| Stone | Terrain anchor | Narrative note |
|-------|---------------|----------------|
| I | Forest edge, north of centre | Deepest in the trees; the most overgrown |
| II | Hilltop overlooking starting valley | Visible from a rise; the easiest to find first |
| III | Beside the Sacred Spring | Two discoveries at once |
| IV | Near the High Cairn, mountain approach | Requires crossing difficult terrain |
| V | Within the Drowned Temple ruins | The hardest; the most complete memory |

## The Ancient Landmarks

### The Ancient Oak
The oldest living thing on the map. It doesn't look obviously different from other trees — slightly larger, more gnarled, the bark darker and more furrowed. But when you approach, the residue around it is unmistakable: centuries of unconscious reverence have accumulated in the wood. People sat beneath it. They brought problems to it. They didn't know why.

You do.

*"There are things that remember without minds. This tree stood when your first worshipper pressed their forehead to the earth. It grew in the direction your presence indicated. It has been pointing at you for three hundred years."*

### The Sacred Spring
Tucked in a valley fold the fog makes invisible until you're nearly upon it. It doesn't glow. It doesn't shimmer unusually. It simply has a quality of *stillness* that distinguishes it from ordinary water — a place where people once left things. Small stones worn smooth from palms. Handfuls of grain. The agreement was never formalised. It didn't need to be.

*"She came here every season. Brought a stone worn smooth from her palm. She never said a name. She just left the stone and walked away. You felt it land."*

### The Drowned Temple
The last formal place built in your name — now mostly swallowed by a waterlogged depression in the terrain. Broken columns visible through shallow water. The carvings are too worn to read. You feel the shape of the intention behind them anyway: people who understood what you were and tried to give it form. They got it slightly wrong. It didn't matter.

*"They built this wrong. The proportions were off, the carvings clumsy. They argued about it for years. It didn't matter. What mattered was the trying. You loved them for the trying, and that love is still here, crystallised in the stone."*

### The High Cairn
A deliberate pile of stones on a mountain approach — not large, not impressive, but unmistakably intentional. Cairns mark that someone was here, that they wanted to leave a trace. This one was built for you, though the builder would not have been able to explain why they felt the need to pile stones on a mountainside and leave them there.

*"High places. People always build high when they want to reach something. The irony is you were never up there. You were in the stone itself — the solidity, the patience, the ten thousand years of staying. They were closer than they knew when they pressed their palms to the earth."*

## Quests

These are not tracked on a quest log — they are discovered through play. The player should feel they are *finding* these things, not completing objectives.

### Primary — The Shattered Name
Find the five standing stones scattered across the map. Each gives energy and a piece of your name. Three are required to advance; five are the full story.

- Stone I and II are findable without too much exploration
- Stone III requires finding the Sacred Spring first
- Stone IV requires traversing hill terrain to the mountain approach
- Stone V is the most remote — deep in the fog, inside the Drowned Temple

### Secondary — The Ancient Memory
Find the three oldest inanimate landmarks: the Ancient Oak, the Sacred Spring, and the Drowned Temple. None are required, but each gives significant energy and the richest memory fragments in Phase 0. Together they paint a complete portrait of who you were and why you dissolved.

### Ambient — Residue Collection
Collect scattered orbs from across the landscape. These fill the gaps between landmark discoveries and reward thorough exploration of the fog. Higher density near landmarks subtly guides curious players toward points of interest.

## The Challenge of Becoming a Whisper

The transition is not handed to the player. It requires:

1. **Energy threshold:** 100 units
2. **Name threshold:** At least 3 of 5 standing stones found
3. **Real exploration:** Vision radius is only 4 tiles at start. Finding distant landmarks requires meaningful time in the world.
4. **Terrain variety:** The Drowned Temple is water-adjacent. The High Cairn is a mountain approach. The Sacred Spring is in a concealed valley. Reaching them takes different routes.

This means a player who only runs toward the nearest orbs will fall short on either energy or syllables. The phase is designed to reward *exploration for its own sake* — wandering into the fog because you want to see what's there.

---

## The Moment of Transition

When both conditions are met, the world holds still.

The energy doesn't drain — it *ignites*. The particle expands outward in a burst of light. The fog of war peels back in a wide radius all at once as the Whisper's broader perception arrives. The transition animation is longer and more elaborate than any subsequent phase change.

Then, before the ascension tree opens, the first coherent memory returns — not a fragment:

> *"Your name is Aethon. You remember. It means: the one who was here before the naming of things.*
>
> *You remember a woman with grey hair and strong hands sitting at a loom in the dark. She was singing to you without knowing she was singing to you. The song had your name in it, folded into a melody so old it had stopped sounding like language.*
>
> *You remember thinking: this is enough. This is what it means to be a god. Not the temples. Not the ceremonies. This — a woman singing in the dark because the dark is less frightening when you fill it with sound."*

The ascension tree opens immediately after. The player now has 2 sparks and their first real choice: what kind of god will Aethon become?

---

## Tone Notes for Phase 0

Phase 0 should feel like:
- Walking through a landscape that has been waiting for you
- Archaeology without tools — finding things by feel
- Coming home to somewhere you've never been but recognise
- The long intake of breath before speaking

It should *not* feel like:
- A tutorial
- A grind
- A puzzle with a correct solution
- Urgency of any kind

The player is alone in the world and this should feel like a gift, not a limitation.
